➕ Sums and Differences

To add or subtract fractions, get a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators. Keep the denominator.

Example

−1/2 + 3/4 = −2/4 + 3/4 = 1/4

For decimals, line up the decimal points. The integer sign rules still apply: different signs means subtract and use the sign of the number farther from zero.

For mixed numbers, you can change them to improper fractions first: 2 1/3 = 7/3.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Different denominators

2/3 − 5/6

4/6 − 5/6 = −1/6.

Answer: −1/6

Example 2: Negative decimals

−4.7 + 2.35

Different signs: 4.70 − 2.35 = 2.35. −4.7 is farther from zero.

Answer: −2.35

Example 3: Mixed numbers

3 1/4 − 1 2/3

13/4 − 5/3 = 39/12 − 20/12 = 19/12.

Answer: 19/12 or 1 7/12

✖️ Products and Powers

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators. Simplify first by canceling common factors to keep the numbers small.

Example

(3/4)(−8/9): cancel 4 with 8 and 3 with 9 → (1/1)(−2/3) = −2/3

For decimals, multiply like whole numbers, then count the total decimal places in the factors.

A power of a fraction applies to both parts: (2/3)2 = 4/9. Watch the sign: (−1/2)3 = −1/8.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Cancel first

(5/6)(−9/10)

Cancel 5 with 10 (leaves 1 and 2) and 6 with 9 (leaves 2 and 3): (1/2)(−3/2).

Answer: −3/4

Example 2: Decimals

(−0.4)(2.5)

4 × 25 = 100. Two decimal places: 1.00. Different signs.

Answer: −1

Example 3: Power

(−2/5)2

(−2/5)(−2/5) = 4/25.

Answer: 4/25

➗ Quotients and Roots

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal (flip the second fraction). Keep, change, flip:

Example

2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 · 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6

To divide decimals, move the decimal in the divisor to make it a whole number, and move the dividend's decimal the same number of places.

The square root of a fraction is the root of the top over the root of the bottom: √(9/25) = 3/5.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Divide fractions

−3/4 ÷ 9/8

−3/4 · 8/9 = −24/36.

Answer: −2/3

Example 2: Divide decimals

7.2 ÷ 0.08

Move both decimals 2 places: 720 ÷ 8.

Answer: 90

Example 3: Root of a fraction

√(16/49)

√16 = 4 and √49 = 7.

Answer: 4/7

🔌 Evaluating Algebraic Expressions

Evaluate just like before: substitute, then follow the order of operations. Use parentheses around every substituted value, especially fractions and negatives.

Example

4x − 1 when x = −3/4: 4(−3/4) − 1 = −3 − 1 = −4

Formulas are expressions too. The Fahrenheit temperature for a Celsius reading is F = (9/5)C + 32.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Fraction value

6y + 2 when y = 2/3

6(2/3) + 2 = 4 + 2.

Answer: 6

Example 2: Decimal value

a2 − a when a = 0.5

(0.5)2 − 0.5 = 0.25 − 0.5.

Answer: −0.25

Example 3: Formula

F = (9/5)C + 32 when C = 25

(9/5)(25) + 32 = 45 + 32.

Answer: 77°F

🧹 Simplifying Algebraic Expressions

Combine like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients, even when the coefficients are fractions or decimals. Distribute first if there are parentheses.

Examples

1.5x + 2.3x = 3.8x
(1/2)y + (1/3)y = (3/6)y + (2/6)y = (5/6)y

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Decimals

4.2a − 1.7a + 3

4.2 − 1.7 = 2.5.

Answer: 2.5a + 3

Example 2: Fractions

(3/4)x − (1/2)x

3/4 − 2/4 = 1/4.

Answer: (1/4)x

Example 3: Distribute a fraction

(2/3)(6n − 9) + n

4n − 6 + n.

Answer: 5n − 6

⚖️ Solving Equations with Rational Numbers

Solve the same way as before: undo adding or subtracting, then undo multiplying or dividing.

Fraction coefficient

Multiply both sides by the reciprocal.
(2/3)x = 8 → x = 8 · 3/2 = 12

Clear the fractions

Multiply every term by the LCD.
x/4 + 1/2 = 3 → (×4) → x + 2 = 12

With decimals, you can multiply every term by 10 or 100 to get whole numbers.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Reciprocal

(3/5)n = −9

Multiply both sides by 5/3: −9 · 5/3.

Answer: n = −15

Example 2: Decimals

0.4x + 1.2 = 3.6

Subtract 1.2: 0.4x = 2.4. Divide by 0.4.

Answer: x = 6

Example 3: Clear fractions

y/3 − 1/2 = 5/6

Multiply each term by 6: 2y − 3 = 5, so 2y = 8.

Answer: y = 4

📝 Using Equations to Solve Problems

Many real problems use fractions and decimals: money, measurements, and “part of” situations. Use the same steps: define a variable, write an equation, solve, and check.

“Of” means multiply

“2/5 of the class is 12 students” means (2/5)n = 12.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Part of a whole

3/4 of the students went on the field trip. That was 54 students. How many students are there in all?

(3/4)n = 54. Multiply by 4/3.

Answer: 72 students

Example 2: Money

Gas costs $3.25 per gallon. You spend $39. How many gallons did you buy?

3.25g = 39, so g = 39 ÷ 3.25.

Answer: 12 gallons

Example 3: Two steps

A taxi charges $2.50 plus $1.75 per mile. The fare was $16.50. How many miles?

2.50 + 1.75m = 16.50. 1.75m = 14.

Answer: 8 miles

🎯 Practice

🚀 Operations with Scientific Notation

To multiply numbers in scientific notation, multiply the decimal parts and add the exponents. To divide, divide the decimal parts and subtract the exponents.

Fix the answer

If the decimal part isn't between 1 and 10, adjust it. 12 × 105 = 1.2 × 106.

To add or subtract, the exponents must be the same first. Then add or subtract the decimal parts.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Multiply

(3 × 104)(2 × 105)

3 · 2 = 6. 4 + 5 = 9.

Answer: 6 × 109

Example 2: Divide

(8.4 × 107) ÷ (2 × 103)

8.4 ÷ 2 = 4.2. 7 − 3 = 4.

Answer: 4.2 × 104

Example 3: Adjust

(5 × 103)(4 × 102)

5 · 4 = 20, so 20 × 105 = 2.0 × 106.

Answer: 2 × 106
