↔️ Opposites and Absolute Value

Two numbers are opposites if they are the same distance from zero on a number line but on different sides. The opposite of 6 is −6, and the opposite of −6 is 6. Zero is its own opposite.

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero. Distance is never negative, so absolute value is never negative. We write it with bars: |−8| = 8 and |8| = 8.

Remember

A minus sign outside the bars stays: −|5| = −5. The bars only work on what's inside them.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Absolute value

|−14|

−14 is 14 steps from zero.

Answer: 14

Example 2: Opposite of a negative

−(−9)

This means “the opposite of −9.” The opposite of −9 is 9.

Answer: 9

Example 3: Comparing

Which is greater: −7 or −2?

On a number line, −2 is farther right than −7. Farther right is greater.

Answer: −2 is greater

➕ Adding and Subtracting Integers

On a number line, adding a positive moves you right and adding a negative moves you left.

Same signs

Add the absolute values. Keep the sign. −4 + (−5) = −9

Different signs

Subtract the absolute values. Use the sign of the number farther from zero. −9 + 4 = −5

To subtract, add the opposite. Keep the first number, change subtraction to addition, and change the sign of the second number: 3 − 8 = 3 + (−8) = −5.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Two negatives

−12 + (−7)

Same signs: 12 + 7 = 19, keep the negative.

Answer: −19

Example 2: Different signs

−15 + 6

Subtract 15 − 6 = 9. The −15 is farther from zero, so the answer is negative.

Answer: −9

Example 3: Subtracting a negative

4 − (−10)

Add the opposite: 4 + 10.

Answer: 14

✖️ Multiplying and Dividing Integers

Multiply or divide the numbers like normal, then decide the sign.

Same signs → positive

(−6)(−3) = 18    −20 ÷ (−4) = 5

Different signs → negative

(−6)(3) = −18    20 ÷ (−4) = −5

With more than two numbers, count the negatives. An even number of negatives gives a positive answer, and an odd number gives a negative answer.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Two negatives

(−9)(−7)

9 × 7 = 63. Same signs, so positive.

Answer: 63

Example 2: Dividing

−56 ÷ 8

56 ÷ 8 = 7. Different signs, so negative.

Answer: −7

Example 3: Three factors

(−2)(−5)(−3)

2 × 5 × 3 = 30. Three negatives is odd, so negative.

Answer: −30

🧩 Properties of Addition and Multiplication

These properties are always true, so you can use them to make problems easier to do in your head.

Commutative (order)

a + b = b + a
a · b = b · a

Associative (grouping)

(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
(a · b) · c = a · (b · c)

Identity

a + 0 = a    a · 1 = a

Inverse & zero

a + (−a) = 0    a · 0 = 0

Subtraction and division are not commutative: 9 − 4 is not the same as 4 − 9.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Reorder to make tens

17 + 28 + 3

Commutative: 17 + 3 + 28 = 20 + 28.

Answer: 48

Example 2: Regroup to multiply

(−25)(7)(4)

Group −25 and 4 first: (−25 · 4) · 7 = −100 · 7.

Answer: −700

Example 3: Name the property

−13 + 13 = 0

A number plus its opposite is zero.

Answer: Inverse property of addition

🔢 Exponents and Properties of Powers

An exponent tells how many times to use the base as a factor. In 25, the base is 2 and the exponent is 5: 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 · 2 = 32.

Watch the parentheses: (−3)2 = (−3)(−3) = 9, but −32 = −(3 · 3) = −9.

Multiplying powers

Same base? Add exponents.
x3 · x4 = x7

Dividing powers

Same base? Subtract exponents.
x9 ÷ x2 = x7

Power of a power

Multiply exponents.
(x2)5 = x10

Zero exponent

Any nonzero number to the 0 power is 1.
70 = 1

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Negative base

(−2)3

(−2)(−2)(−2): three negatives, so the answer is negative. 2 · 2 · 2 = 8.

Answer: −8

Example 2: Product of powers

52 · 54

Same base, so add the exponents: 2 + 4 = 6.

Answer: 56

Example 3: Power of a power

(a3)4

Multiply the exponents: 3 · 4 = 12.

Answer: a12

🌱 Square Roots and Cube Roots

A square root undoes squaring. √49 = 7 because 72 = 49. A cube root undoes cubing. ∛27 = 3 because 33 = 27.

Numbers like 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144 are perfect squares. Learn them by heart; they make roots fast.

Negatives inside

√(−16) is not an integer (no integer times itself is negative). But ∛(−8) = −2, because (−2)3 = −8.

Estimating

√50 is between √49 = 7 and √64 = 8, and it's very close to 7.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Square root

√144

12 · 12 = 144.

Answer: 12

Example 2: Negative of a root

−√81

√81 = 9, then the minus sign in front makes it −9.

Answer: −9

Example 3: Estimate

√30 is between which two integers?

25 < 30 < 36, so √30 is between 5 and 6.

Answer: 5 and 6

🪜 Order of Operations

When a problem has more than one operation, work in this order:

  1. Grouping symbols: parentheses, brackets, fraction bars, absolute value bars, and roots
  2. Exponents
  3. Multiply and divide, left to right
  4. Add and subtract, left to right

Watch out

Multiplication does not always come before division. Do whichever comes first going left to right. The same goes for addition and subtraction.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Exponents before multiplying

3 + 2 · 42

42 = 16. Then 2 · 16 = 32. Then 3 + 32.

Answer: 35

Example 2: Left to right

24 ÷ 6 · 2

Go left to right: 24 ÷ 6 = 4, then 4 · 2.

Answer: 8

Example 3: With negatives

−10 + (3 − 7)2 ÷ 2

(3 − 7) = −4. (−4)2 = 16. 16 ÷ 2 = 8. −10 + 8.

Answer: −2
