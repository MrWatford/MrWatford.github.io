🗺️ What this page covers

Perimeter measures the distance around a figure, area measures the space it covers, surface area measures the total area of a solid's outside, and volume measures how much a solid holds. Here we use formulas for all four, including circles, cylinders, cones, and spheres, and see how size changes affect similar figures.

⭕ Perimeter and Circumference

The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of its side lengths. For a rectangle, P = 2l + 2w.

The perimeter of a circle is called its circumference. The radius (r) goes from the center to the edge, and the diameter (d) goes all the way across, so d = 2r.

Circumference

C = πd or C = 2πr. We use π ≈ 3.14.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Rectangle

Perimeter of a 12 m by 5 m rectangle

2(12) + 2(5) = 24 + 10.

Answer: 34 m

Example 2: Circle from radius

Circumference when r = 7 cm

2 × 3.14 × 7 = 43.96.

Answer: 43.96 cm (or 14π cm)

Example 3: Find the diameter

A circle's circumference is 62.8 in. Find its diameter.

62.8 = 3.14d, so d = 62.8 ÷ 3.14.

Answer: 20 in.

⬛ Area

Area is the number of square units a figure covers.

Rectangle

A = lw

Parallelogram

A = bh (h is the straight-up height)

Triangle

A = (1/2)bh

Trapezoid

A = (1/2)(b1 + b2)h

Circle

A = πr2. Square the radius first, then multiply by π.

For an odd shape, split it into simple shapes and add their areas.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Triangle

Base 10 ft, height 7 ft

(1/2)(10)(7).

Answer: 35 ft²

Example 2: Trapezoid

Bases 4 and 10, height 6

(1/2)(4 + 10)(6) = (1/2)(14)(6).

Answer: 42 square units

Example 3: Circle

Radius 3 m

3.14 × 3² = 3.14 × 9.

Answer: 28.26 m² (or 9π m²)

🔍 Lengths and Areas of Similar Figures

When a figure is enlarged or shrunk by a scale factor k:

Lengths

Every length (sides, perimeter, circumference) is multiplied by k.

Areas

Every area is multiplied by k2.

Why k2? Area uses two lengths multiplied together, and each one grew by k. Double the sides of a square and its area becomes 4 times as big.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Perimeter

A triangle has perimeter 15. It's enlarged by a scale factor of 3. New perimeter?

Lengths multiply by 3.

Answer: 45

Example 2: Area

A rectangle has area 12 cm². It's enlarged by a scale factor of 3. New area?

Areas multiply by 32 = 9.

Answer: 108 cm²

Example 3: Find k

Two similar squares have areas 25 and 100. What is the scale factor?

Area ratio 100/25 = 4 = k2, so k = 2.

Answer: 2

🎁 Surface Areas of Prisms and Cylinders

Surface area is the total area of all the outside surfaces of a solid. Imagine unfolding it into a flat pattern (a net) and adding the areas.

Rectangular prism

SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh

Cylinder

SA = 2πr2 + 2πrh
(two circles + the label wrapped around)

A cube with edge s has SA = 6s2.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Rectangular prism

2 by 3 by 4

2(6) + 2(8) + 2(12) = 12 + 16 + 24.

Answer: 52 square units

Example 2: Cube

Edge 5 cm

6 × 5² = 6 × 25.

Answer: 150 cm²

Example 3: Cylinder

r = 3, h = 5

2π(9) + 2π(3)(5) = 18π + 30π = 48π ≈ 150.72.

Answer: 150.72 (or 48π)

🔺 Surface Areas of Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres

Pyramids and cones come to a point. Their side area uses the slant height (ℓ), measured along the slanted side, not straight up.

Regular pyramid

SA = B + (1/2)Pℓ
B = base area, P = base perimeter

Cone

SA = πr2 + πrℓ

Sphere

SA = 4πr2

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Square pyramid

Base edge 6, slant height 4

B = 36. P = 24. SA = 36 + (1/2)(24)(4) = 36 + 48.

Answer: 84 square units

Example 2: Cone

r = 3, slant height 5

π(9) + π(3)(5) = 9π + 15π = 24π ≈ 75.36.

Answer: 75.36 (or 24π)

Example 3: Sphere

r = 3

4π(9) = 36π ≈ 113.04.

Answer: 113.04 (or 36π)

🧊 Volumes of Prisms and Cylinders

Volume is the number of cubic units a solid holds. For any prism or cylinder:

V = Bh

B is the area of the base and h is the height.

Rectangular prism

V = lwh

Cylinder

V = πr2h

For a triangular prism, B is the triangle's area: (1/2)bh.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Rectangular prism

4 by 5 by 6

4 × 5 × 6.

Answer: 120 cubic units

Example 2: Cylinder

r = 2, h = 10

3.14 × 4 × 10 = 125.6.

Answer: 125.6 (or 40π)

Example 3: Triangular prism

Triangle base 6 and height 4; prism length 10

B = (1/2)(6)(4) = 12. V = 12 × 10.

Answer: 120 cubic units

🍦 Volumes of Pyramids, Cones, and Spheres

A pyramid holds exactly one-third as much as a prism with the same base and height. A cone holds one-third as much as a matching cylinder.

Pyramid

V = (1/3)Bh

Cone

V = (1/3)πr2h

Sphere

V = (4/3)πr3

Remember: h is the straight-up height, not the slant height.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Pyramid

Square base 6 by 6, height 5

(1/3)(36)(5) = 60.

Answer: 60 cubic units

Example 2: Cone

r = 3, h = 4

(1/3)(3.14)(9)(4) = 12π ≈ 37.68.

Answer: 37.68 (or 12π)

Example 3: Sphere

r = 3

(4/3)(3.14)(27) = 36π ≈ 113.04.

Answer: 113.04 (or 36π)
