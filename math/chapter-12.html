📏 Statistical Measures

Mean (average)

Add all the values and divide by how many there are.

Median (middle)

Put the values in order. It's the middle one, or the average of the two middle ones.

Mode (most often)

The value that appears most. There can be more than one, or none.

Range (spread)

Greatest value minus least value.

An outlier is a value far from the rest. Outliers pull the mean toward them, so the median is often a better “typical” value when there's an outlier.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Mean

4, 8, 6, 10, 7

4 + 8 + 6 + 10 + 7 = 35. 35 ÷ 5.

Answer: 7

Example 2: Median, even count

12, 3, 9, 5

In order: 3, 5, 9, 12. Middle two: 5 and 9. (5 + 9) ÷ 2.

Answer: 7

Example 3: Score needed

Test scores 86, 92, 88. What score on the 4th test makes the mean 90?

4 tests × 90 = 360 total. 360 − 266.

Answer: 94

📦 Illustrating Data

A stem-and-leaf plot splits each number into a stem (tens digit) and a leaf (ones digit). The row “3 | 1 4 8” means 31, 34, and 38.

A box plot (box-and-whisker plot) shows the five-number summary:

  1. Minimum (least value)
  2. Lower quartile, Q1: the median of the lower half
  3. Median
  4. Upper quartile, Q3: the median of the upper half
  5. Maximum (greatest value)

Interquartile range (IQR)

IQR = Q3 − Q1. It measures the spread of the middle half of the data.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Five-number summary

2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16

Median: (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9. Lower half 2, 4, 6, 8 → Q1 = 5. Upper half 10, 12, 14, 16 → Q3 = 13.

Answer: 2, 5, 9, 13, 16

Example 2: IQR

Use the data above.

Q3 − Q1 = 13 − 5.

Answer: 8

Example 3: Stem-and-leaf

Rows: 4 | 2 7 and 5 | 0 3 9. What is the greatest value?

The last row is 50, 53, 59.

Answer: 59

📊 Frequency Tables and Histograms

A frequency table counts how many data values fall in each interval. A histogram is a bar graph of that table: the bars touch, and each bar's height is the frequency.

Test scores for one class
ScoreFrequency
60–693
70–797
80–898
90–992

Intervals must be the same size and must not overlap.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Total

How many students are in the class? (table above)

3 + 7 + 8 + 2.

Answer: 20

Example 2: Most common

Which interval has the most scores?

The tallest bar is 80–89 with 8.

Answer: 80–89

Example 3: Percent

What percent scored 90 or higher?

2 out of 20 = 0.10.

Answer: 10%

✳️ Scatterplots

A scatterplot graphs pairs of data as points to show whether two variables are related.

Positive correlation

As one goes up, the other tends to go up. Points rise left to right.

Negative correlation

As one goes up, the other tends to go down. Points fall left to right.

No correlation

No clear pattern.

Outlier

A point far away from the rest.

A correlation doesn't prove that one thing causes the other.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Positive

Height and arm span

Taller people usually have longer arm spans.

Answer: positive

Example 2: Negative

A car's age and its value

Older cars are usually worth less.

Answer: negative

Example 3: None

Shoe size and birthday month

No reason for a pattern.

Answer: no correlation

📈 Trend Lines

A trend line (line of best fit) is a straight line drawn through the middle of the points on a scatterplot, with about as many points above it as below.

Write its equation as y = mx + b, then use it to predict. The slope tells how much y changes for each 1 unit of x.

Example

Plant height y (cm) after x weeks: y = 2.5x + 4. After 6 weeks: 2.5(6) + 4 = 19 cm. The plant grows about 2.5 cm per week.

Be careful predicting far beyond the data. A trend may not continue forever.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Predict

y = 3x + 10 gives sales y for x days. Predict day 8.

3(8) + 10.

Answer: 34

Example 2: Slope meaning

y = 15x + 20 gives cost y for x hours. What does 15 mean?

Each extra hour adds $15.

Answer: $15 per hour

Example 3: Find x

y = 4x + 2. When is y = 30?

4x + 2 = 30, so 4x = 28.

Answer: x = 7

🧮 Two-Way Frequency Tables

A two-way table sorts data by two categories at the same time. The totals are in the last row and column.

Students surveyed: do you play an instrument?
PlaysDoesn't playTotal
7th grade181230
8th grade22830
Total402060

A relative frequency is a count divided by a total, written as a fraction, decimal, or percent.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Read a cell

How many 8th graders play an instrument?

Row “8th grade,” column “Plays.”

Answer: 22

Example 2: Total

How many students play an instrument?

The total of the “Plays” column.

Answer: 40

Example 3: Relative frequency

What fraction of 7th graders play?

18 out of 30 7th graders = 18/30.

Answer: 3/5

🎲 Probability

Probability is a number from 0 to 1 that tells how likely an event is. 0 means impossible, 1 means certain, and 1/2 means equally likely to happen or not.

Theoretical probability

P(event) = number of favorable outcomes ÷ number of possible outcomes

Complement

P(not A) = 1 − P(A)

Experimental probability

Based on what actually happened: times it happened ÷ total trials.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Number cube

P(rolling a number greater than 4)

5 or 6: 2 favorable out of 6.

Answer: 1/3

Example 2: Marbles

A bag has 3 red, 5 blue, and 2 green marbles. P(not blue)

5 of 10 are blue, so P(not blue) = 1 − 5/10.

Answer: 1/2

Example 3: Experimental

A coin landed heads 18 times in 30 flips. Experimental P(heads)?

18/30.

Answer: 3/5

🃏 Probabilities of Compound Events

A compound event combines two or more events, like flipping a coin and rolling a number cube.

Counting principle

If one choice has m options and another has n options, there are m × n outcomes in all.

Independent events

One doesn't affect the other.
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

Dependent events

The first changes the second (like drawing without putting it back).
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B after A)

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Counting

4 shirts and 3 pairs of pants. How many outfits?

4 × 3.

Answer: 12

Example 2: Independent

P(heads and rolling a 6)

1/2 × 1/6.

Answer: 1/12

Example 3: Dependent

A bag has 4 red and 6 blue. Draw two without replacing. P(red, then red)

4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90.

Answer: 2/15
