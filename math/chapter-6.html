🔄 Forms of Percents

Percent means “out of 100.” 45% means 45 out of 100, or 45/100.

Percent → decimal

Move the decimal 2 places left.
38% = 0.38   7% = 0.07   150% = 1.5

Decimal → percent

Move the decimal 2 places right.
0.6 = 60%   0.025 = 2.5%

Percent → fraction

Write it over 100 and simplify.
35% = 35/100 = 7/20

Fraction → percent

Divide to get a decimal, then move 2 places right.
3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Percent to decimal

Write 4.5% as a decimal.

Move the decimal 2 places left.

Answer: 0.045

Example 2: Fraction to percent

Write 7/25 as a percent.

7/25 = 28/100.

Answer: 28%

Example 3: Percent to fraction

Write 80% as a fraction.

80/100. The GCF is 20.

Answer: 4/5

⚖️ Solving Percent Equations

Every percent problem fits one equation:

part = percent × whole

“What is 30% of 80?” → p = 0.30 × 80 = 24

Translate the words: is means =, of means ×, and what is your variable. Write the percent as a decimal before you solve.

  • Find the part: 15% of 60 is what? → 0.15 · 60 = 9
  • Find the percent: 12 is what percent of 48? → 12 = p · 48, so p = 0.25 = 25%
  • Find the whole: 18 is 40% of what? → 18 = 0.4w, so w = 45

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Find the part

What is 35% of 120?

0.35 × 120.

Answer: 42

Example 2: Find the percent

27 is what percent of 90?

27 = p · 90, so p = 27 ÷ 90 = 0.3.

Answer: 30%

Example 3: Find the whole

16 is 20% of what number?

16 = 0.2w, so w = 16 ÷ 0.2.

Answer: 80

📊 Using Percents

In word problems, first decide what you're looking for: the part, the percent, or the whole. Then use part = percent × whole.

Mr. WatfordBreak a hard problem into smaller pieces. Find one piece, then use it to find the next. 10% is a great mental math starting point: just move the decimal one place left!

Mental math with 10%

10% of 70 = 7, so 20% of 70 = 14, and 5% of 70 = 3.5.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Survey

In a survey of 200 students, 38% chose pizza. How many chose pizza?

0.38 × 200.

Answer: 76 students

Example 2: Test score

You got 42 points out of 48. What percent is that?

42 ÷ 48 = 0.875.

Answer: 87.5%

Example 3: Sales tax

A $40 jacket has 6% sales tax. What is the total cost?

Tax: 0.06 × 40 = $2.40. Total: 40 + 2.40.

Answer: $42.40

🏷️ Discount and Markup

Discount (price goes down)

discount = rate × original price
sale price = original − discount

Markup (price goes up)

markup = rate × store's cost
selling price = cost + markup

Shortcut: a 25% discount means you pay 75%, so the sale price is 0.75 × original. A 40% markup means the selling price is 1.40 × cost.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Discount

A $80 game is 30% off. What is the sale price?

Discount: 0.30 × 80 = $24. Sale price: 80 − 24.

Answer: $56

Example 2: Markup

A store buys a lamp for $35 and marks it up 60%. What is the selling price?

Markup: 0.60 × 35 = $21. Price: 35 + 21.

Answer: $56

Example 3: Find the rate

A $50 shirt is on sale for $40. What is the discount rate?

The discount is $10. 10 ÷ 50 = 0.2.

Answer: 20%

🍽️ Tips and Commission

A tip is extra money for service, figured as a percent of the bill. Commission is money a salesperson earns as a percent of what they sell.

Same equation

tip = rate × bill    commission = rate × sales

Tip trick: for 15%, find 10% and add half of it. For 20%, find 10% and double it.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Tip

A meal costs $42. You leave a 20% tip. What is the total?

Tip: 10% is $4.20, so 20% is $8.40. Total: 42 + 8.40.

Answer: $50.40

Example 2: Commission

A salesperson earns 6% commission on $3,500 in sales. How much commission?

0.06 × 3,500.

Answer: $210

Example 3: Salary plus commission

Maya earns $400 a week plus 5% of her sales. She sold $2,000 this week. What did she earn?

Commission: 0.05 × 2,000 = $100. Total: 400 + 100.

Answer: $500

🏦 Interest

Interest is money paid for using money. Banks pay you interest on savings, and you pay interest on a loan.

Simple interest: I = prt

I = interest, p = principal (starting amount), r = yearly rate as a decimal, t = time in years

The total amount is the principal plus interest: A = p + I. If the time is in months, divide by 12 to change it to years: 6 months = 0.5 year.

Mr. WatfordProverbs 22:7 reminds us that the borrower is servant to the lender. Saving pays you interest; borrowing costs you interest!

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Find the interest

$500 saved at 4% for 3 years

I = 500 × 0.04 × 3.

Answer: $60

Example 2: Find the total

$1,200 borrowed at 7% for 2 years. What is the total paid back?

I = 1,200 × 0.07 × 2 = $168. Total: 1,200 + 168.

Answer: $1,368

Example 3: Months

$800 at 5% for 6 months

6 months = 0.5 year. I = 800 × 0.05 × 0.5.

Answer: $20

📈 Percent Change

percent change = amount of change ÷ original amount

Always divide by the original (starting) amount, not the new one.

If the amount went up, it's a percent increase. If it went down, it's a percent decrease.

You can also go the other way: a 10% increase on 50 is 50 + 0.10(50) = 55, or just 1.10 × 50.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Increase

A plant grew from 20 cm to 26 cm. Find the percent change.

Change: 6. 6 ÷ 20 = 0.3.

Answer: 30% increase

Example 2: Decrease

A price dropped from $75 to $60. Find the percent change.

Change: 15. 15 ÷ 75 = 0.2.

Answer: 20% decrease

Example 3: Find the new amount

A town of 8,000 people grows by 5%. What is the new population?

1.05 × 8,000.

Answer: 8,400

🗺️ Scales

A scale compares a length in a drawing, map, or model to the real length. For example, 1 inch : 10 feet means 1 inch on the drawing stands for 10 feet in real life.

Use a proportion

drawing / real = drawing / real
1/10 = 3.5/x → x = 35 feet

A scale factor compares lengths with the same units. A 1:24 model car is 1/24 the size of the real car.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Map

Scale 1 cm : 15 km. Two cities are 6 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they really?

1/15 = 6/x, so x = 90.

Answer: 90 km

Example 2: Blueprint

Scale 1/4 inch : 1 foot. A room is 3 inches long on the plan. How long is the room?

3 ÷ 1/4 = 12 quarter inches, so 12 feet.

Answer: 12 feet

Example 3: Model

A model plane uses a 1:48 scale. The model is 15 inches long. How long is the real plane, in inches?

15 × 48.

Answer: 720 inches (60 feet)
