🗺️ What this chapter covers

A rational number is any number that can be written as a fraction of two integers, like 3/4, −5, or 0.6. 🌳 Prime Factorization

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, …). A composite number has more than two factors. The number 1 is neither.

The prime factorization of a number writes it as a product of primes. Use a factor tree: split the number into any two factors, then keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime.

Example

60 → 6 · 10 → 2 · 3 · 2 · 5. In order with exponents: 60 = 22 · 3 · 5

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Factor tree

Find the prime factorization of 48.

48 = 6 · 8 = (2 · 3)(2 · 2 · 2). That's four 2s and one 3.

Answer: 24 · 3

Example 2: Prime or composite?

Is 51 prime or composite?

51 = 3 · 17, so it has factors other than 1 and 51.

Answer: composite

Example 3: With variables

Factor 18x2 completely.

18 = 2 · 3 · 3 and x2 = x · x.

Answer: 2 · 3 · 3 · x · x

🤝 Greatest Common Factor

The greatest common factor (GCF) is the largest number that is a factor of two or more numbers.

Method 1: List factors

12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
GCF = 6

Method 2: Prime factors

12 = 2 · 2 · 3
18 = 2 · 3 · 3
Shared primes: 2 · 3 = 6

If the GCF of two numbers is 1, they are called relatively prime.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Two numbers

GCF of 24 and 36

24 = 2 · 2 · 2 · 3 and 36 = 2 · 2 · 3 · 3. They share 2 · 2 · 3.

Answer: 12

Example 2: Three numbers

GCF of 15, 45, and 60

15 = 3 · 5. Both 45 and 60 are divisible by 15.

Answer: 15

Example 3: Real life

You have 28 pencils and 42 erasers. You want identical kits with nothing left over. What is the greatest number of kits?

Find the GCF of 28 and 42.

Answer: 14 kits

🔔 Least Common Multiple

The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

Method 1: List multiples

6: 6, 12, 18, 24
9: 9, 18, 27
LCM = 18

Method 2: Prime factors

Use each prime the most times it appears in either number.
6 = 2 · 3, 9 = 3 · 3
LCM = 2 · 3 · 3 = 18

The LCM of denominators is the least common denominator, which you need to add fractions.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Two numbers

LCM of 8 and 12

Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36. Is 24 a multiple of 8? Yes.

Answer: 24

Example 2: Using primes

LCM of 10 and 15

10 = 2 · 5, 15 = 3 · 5. Use 2, 3, and 5 once each.

Answer: 30

Example 3: Real life

Hot dogs come in packs of 10 and buns in packs of 8. What is the fewest of each you can buy to have the same number?

LCM of 10 and 8.

Answer: 40

🍕 Rational Numbers

A rational number can be written as a/b, where a and b are integers and b is not 0. Integers, fractions, terminating decimals, and repeating decimals are all rational.

To write a fraction in simplest form, divide the top and bottom by their GCF: 18/24 → divide by 6 → 3/4.

Comparing fractions

Rewrite them with a common denominator, or use cross products. To compare 3/5 and 2/3: 3 · 3 = 9 and 5 · 2 = 10. Since 9 < 10, 3/5 < 2/3.

A negative fraction can show the sign in three places: −2/3 = (−2)/3 = 2/(−3).

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Simplest form

Simplify 20/35.

The GCF of 20 and 35 is 5. 20 ÷ 5 = 4 and 35 ÷ 5 = 7.

Answer: 4/7

Example 2: Compare

Compare 5/8 and 7/12.

Common denominator 24: 15/24 and 14/24.

Answer: 5/8 > 7/12

Example 3: Negatives

Which is greater: −3/4 or −2/3?

−9/12 and −8/12. −8/12 is closer to zero.

Answer: −2/3 is greater

🔄 Decimal Equivalents

To change a fraction to a decimal, divide the top by the bottom: 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.

If the division ends, it's a terminating decimal. If a digit or group of digits repeats forever, it's a repeating decimal, shown with a bar: 1/3 = 0.333… = 0.3.

Decimal to fraction

Read the place value and simplify. 0.45 is 45 hundredths = 45/100 = 9/20.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Terminating

Write 7/20 as a decimal.

7 ÷ 20 = 0.35. (Or: 7/20 = 35/100.)

Answer: 0.35

Example 2: Repeating

Write 5/11 as a decimal.

5 ÷ 11 = 0.454545… The 45 repeats.

Answer: 0.45

Example 3: Decimal to fraction

Write 0.625 as a fraction.

625/1000. The GCF is 125.

Answer: 5/8

⚖️ Ratios and Proportions

A ratio compares two quantities. The ratio of 3 cats to 5 dogs can be written 3 to 5, 3:5, or 3/5. A rate compares different units, like 120 miles in 2 hours. A unit rate has 1 in the bottom: 60 miles per hour.

A proportion says two ratios are equal: 2/3 = 8/12.

Cross products

In a proportion, the cross products are equal. To solve x/6 = 10/15: 15x = 60, so x = 4.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Simplify a ratio

12 boys to 18 girls

Divide both by 6.

Answer: 2 : 3

Example 2: Unit rate

$4.50 for 3 pounds of apples

4.50 ÷ 3 = 1.50.

Answer: $1.50 per pound

Example 3: Solve a proportion

5/8 = n/40

8n = 200, so n = 25.

Answer: n = 25

♾️ The Real Number System

The real numbers are sorted into groups that fit inside each other:

  • Natural numbers: 1, 2, 3, … (counting numbers)
  • Whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, …
  • Integers: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …
  • Rational numbers: anything that can be written as a fraction of integers, including terminating and repeating decimals
  • Irrational numbers: decimals that never end and never repeat, like π and √2

Square roots

The square root of a perfect square is rational (√49 = 7). The square root of any other whole number is irrational (√50 = 7.0710678…).

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Classify

−12

It's an integer, so it's also rational. It isn't whole because it's negative.

Answer: integer, rational

Example 2: Classify

√36

√36 = 6, which is natural, whole, an integer, and rational.

Answer: natural, whole, integer, rational

Example 3: Classify

√15

15 isn't a perfect square, so its root never ends or repeats.

Answer: irrational
