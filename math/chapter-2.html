🗺️ What this page covers

🔌 Evaluating Expressions

A variable is a letter that stands for a number. To evaluate an expression, replace each variable with its value, then simplify using the order of operations.

Tip

Put the substituted number in parentheses, especially when it's negative. If x = −3, then x2 = (−3)2 = 9.

A number written right next to a variable means multiply: 5y means 5 · y.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: One variable

4n − 9 when n = 6

4(6) − 9 = 24 − 9.

Answer: 15

Example 2: A negative value

x2 + 2x when x = −5

(−5)2 + 2(−5) = 25 + (−10).

Answer: 15

Example 3: Two variables

(a + b) ÷ 3 when a = 10, b = 8

(10 + 8) ÷ 3 = 18 ÷ 3.

Answer: 6

🎁 The Distributive Property

The distributive property says a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply the number outside by each term inside.

It works with subtraction too: a(b − c) = ab − ac. Watch the signs when the outside number is negative: −2(x − 5) = −2x + 10.

Mental math trick

6 · 49 = 6(50 − 1) = 300 − 6 = 294

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Basic

4(x + 7)

4 · x + 4 · 7.

Answer: 4x + 28

Example 2: Negative outside

−3(2y − 5)

−3 · 2y = −6y, and −3 · (−5) = +15.

Answer: −6y + 15

Example 3: Mental math

8 · 102

8(100 + 2) = 800 + 16.

Answer: 816

🧹 Simplifying Expressions

Terms are the parts of an expression separated by + or −. Like terms have exactly the same variable part: 3x and −8x are like terms, but 3x and 3x2 are not.

To combine like terms, add or subtract their coefficients (the numbers in front) and keep the variable part the same. If there are parentheses, distribute first.

Keep the sign with its term

In 9 − 4x + 2x, the terms are 9, −4x, and +2x. So it simplifies to 9 − 2x.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Combine like terms

6x + 3 + 2x − 8

6x + 2x = 8x. 3 − 8 = −5.

Answer: 8x − 5

Example 2: Distribute first

3(y + 4) − 5y

3y + 12 − 5y. Then 3y − 5y = −2y.

Answer: −2y + 12

Example 3: Two variables

4a + 2b − a + 6b

4a − a = 3a. 2b + 6b = 8b.

Answer: 3a + 8b

💬 Translating Word Phrases

Many word problems start by turning a phrase into an expression. Look for key words:

Add

sum, plus, more than, increased by, total

Subtract

difference, minus, less than, decreased by, fewer

Multiply

product, times, twice, double, of

Divide

quotient, divided by, half, per, split

Watch out for “less than”

“5 less than x” means x − 5, not 5 − x. You start with x and take 5 away.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: More than

7 more than a number n

Start with n and add 7.

Answer: n + 7

Example 2: Less than

4 less than twice a number x

Twice x is 2x. Then take 4 away.

Answer: 2x − 4

Example 3: Quotient

the quotient of a number y and 3, increased by 10

y ÷ 3, then add 10.

Answer: y/3 + 10

🚀 Scientific Notation

Scientific notation writes a number as a number from 1 up to (but not including) 10, times a power of 10. For example, 4,500,000 = 4.5 × 106.

Big numbers

Move the decimal left until one nonzero digit is in front. The number of moves is a positive exponent.
32,000 = 3.2 × 104

Small numbers

Move the decimal right until one nonzero digit is in front. The number of moves is a negative exponent.
0.0061 = 6.1 × 10−3

To go back to standard form, move the decimal right for a positive exponent and left for a negative exponent.

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Large number

Write 93,000,000 in scientific notation.

Move the decimal 7 places left to get 9.3.

Answer: 9.3 × 107

Example 2: Small number

Write 0.00042 in scientific notation.

Move the decimal 4 places right to get 4.2.

Answer: 4.2 × 10−4

Example 3: Back to standard form

6.08 × 105

Move the decimal 5 places right, adding zeros.

Answer: 608,000

🎯 Estimating

An estimate is a close answer you can find quickly. It helps you check whether an exact answer is reasonable.

  • Rounding: round each number to a place that's easy to work with. 487 + 312 ≈ 500 + 300 = 800.
  • Compatible numbers: pick nearby numbers that divide nicely. 362 ÷ 9 ≈ 360 ÷ 9 = 40.
  • Front-end: use the first digit of each number, then adjust.

Mr. WatfordIf your calculator says 4,000 and your estimate says 40, something went wrong. Check your work!

✏️ Worked examples

Example 1: Rounding

Estimate 68 × 31.

Round to 70 × 30.

Answer: about 2,100

Example 2: Compatible numbers

Estimate 418 ÷ 7.

420 is close to 418 and divides evenly by 7.

Answer: about 60

Example 3: Money

You buy items for $4.89, $7.15, and $2.98. Estimate the total.

$5 + $7 + $3.

Answer: about $15
