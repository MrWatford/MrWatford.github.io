🫙 Project 1 • Layers in a Jar (required for everyone)

The big idea: Some people say rock layers took a very long time to form. But when Mount St. Helens erupted in 1980, it laid down hundreds of feet of layers in just days! In this experiment, you'll watch layers form in minutes, just like the Flood could have done on a much bigger scale.

What you need

  • Clear jar with a lid
  • Sand
  • Garden dirt
  • Small rocks or gravel
  • Water
  • 3–5 tiny toy animals or shells
  • Phone or camera

💡 Use a jar with a tight lid so it doesn't leak!
💡 Ask a parent before using garden dirt.

Steps

  1. Fill it. Fill the jar halfway with sand, dirt, and rocks. Drop in your "fossils."
  2. Add water. Fill with water until almost full. Screw the lid on tight!
  3. Shake it. Shake hard for 30 seconds. Then set it down and don't touch it.
  4. Snap pics. Take pictures at 1 minute, 10 minutes, and 1 day. Count the layers!

✏️ Your write-up (about half a page)

Answer these 4 questions:

  1. How many layers formed?
  2. How long did it take?
  3. Where did your "fossils" end up?
  4. How does this connect to the Flood or Mount St. Helens?

Turn in for Project 1Bring your jar (or your 3 pictures) and your write-up. Be ready to show the class!

A Strata in a Cup 🥤

Build rock layers with a "tree" standing up through all of them, like a real polystrate fossil. A tree can't stand for ages waiting to be buried, so the layers had to form fast!

You need

Clear cup • 3–5 colors of sand • pretzel rod or straw • index card

Steps

  1. Stand your "tree" in the middle of the cup.
  2. Pour in 1 inch of sand. Add the next color on top. Make at least 4 layers.
  3. Make sure the tree goes through every layer.
  4. On the card, label the layers and write 3–4 sentences on why this shows fast burial.

B Missing Link Myth-Busters 🔍

Be a detective! Pick one famous "missing link" and find out if it was really an in-between creature.

Pick one

  • Piltdown Man: human skull + ape jaw
  • Nebraska Man: made from a pig's tooth!
  • Archaeoraptor: two fossils glued together
  • Lucy: most likely an extinct ape
  • Neanderthals: 100% human

Make a poster with 3 parts

  1. The Claim: what people said it was
  2. The Evidence: what scientists found out
  3. The Verdict: real or BUSTED?

Add a title, pictures, and color. Use textbook pp. 141–151.

C Fossil News Report 🎤

You're the reporter! Break a real fossil story like it's on TV.

Pick one story

  • 1938: A fish called "extinct," the coelacanth, is caught alive!
  • 1953: The Piltdown "ape-man" is exposed as a fake.
  • 1980: Mount St. Helens lays down rock layers in days.

Steps

  1. Write a 1–2 minute script: Who? What? When? Where? Why does it matter?
  2. Add a pretend interview with a scientist or eyewitness.
  3. Record a video or perform it live. Props welcome!

Turn in your script too.
