The Earthquake Tower Challenge

Can your team engineer a spaghetti-and-marshmallow building that stays standing when the ground starts to shake?

  • Teams2–3 students
  • HeightAt least 12 in.
  • Shake test3 rounds

💡 Engineer's tips

Mr. WatfordTriangles are stronger than squares. A wide base and a low center of gravity help a building resist shaking.

📋 Building requirements

  • Teams: Work in groups of 2–3. Every member helps build.
  • Materials only: Unlimited spaghetti and marshmallows. Break spaghetti as needed. No tape, glue, or string.
  • Height: The tower must be at least 12 inches (30 cm) tall.
  • Free-standing: It must stand on its own, not taped, held, or leaned on anything.
  • Plan first: Sketch your design before you start building.
  • Test day: No need to finish in one class period. The shake test happens in class the next day.

🔍 Build this, not that

✅ Do this

  • Triangle braces (X's) on every side
  • Wide base, narrow top

❌ Not that

  • Random sticks with no plan
  • Skinny base, heavy clumps up top

📐 Add side support beams

  1. Lean it in. Run a spaghetti beam from high on the tower down at an angle to the table.
  2. Reach out wide. Anchor the bottom with a marshmallow far away from the base.
  3. Every side. Put a support on all four sides so it can't tip any direction.

Mr. WatfordWhy it works: the beams make big triangles that push back when the shaking tries to tip your tower over.

📳 The shake test

In class, each tower faces three 10-second rounds on the shake table.

Round 1

Tremor

Gentle shaking

5 ptsStill standing
Round 2

Quake

Medium shaking

10 ptsStill standing
Round 3

The Big One

Strong shaking

15 ptsStill standing

Mr. WatfordSurvive all three rounds and you're an Earthquake-Proof Engineer! 🏆

💭 Think about it

  1. Why do triangles hold their shape better than squares when they're pushed?
  2. Which part of your tower moved the most during the shake test? How could you make it stronger?
  3. How might engineers use these same ideas to design buildings in places that have earthquakes?
