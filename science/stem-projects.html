STEM Project Center

Design it. Build it. Test it. Improve it.

STEM combines science, technology, engineering, and math to solve problems, test ideas, and build useful things. In these projects, you will use everyday materials to create a solution, measure how well it works, and improve your design. Some challenges are perfect for beginners, while others require more advanced planning and building.

🎬 Watch: The STEM Method

🔤 The four parts of STEM

  • S
    Science

    Understand the forces, materials, energy, and natural processes involved.

  • T
    Technology

    Use or create tools and systems that help solve a problem. Technology does not always mean computers. A ramp, a pulley, or a folded paper tube is technology too.

  • E
    Engineering

    Design, build, test, and improve a solution.

  • M
    Math

    Measure, calculate, compare results, and use evidence to make decisions.

Mr. WatfordEvery project here uses all four parts. Open any project and look for “How STEM connects” to see exactly how science, technology, engineering, and math each help you succeed.

🧭 How to Complete a STEM Challenge

Engineers use a design process to solve problems. These eight steps will guide you from a problem to a tested, improved solution. You may go back and repeat steps as you learn. That is normal, and it is what real engineers do.

  1. STEP 1

    Ask

    What problem are you solving? Who would use your solution?

  2. STEP 2

    Identify goals and limits

    What must the design accomplish? What materials, space, and time are available?

    Words to know

    Criteria
    What a successful design must do.
    Constraints
    The limits you must work within.

    Example: In the Index-Card Tower, standing on its own for 30 seconds is a criterion. Using only 20 index cards and a set length of tape are constraints.

  3. STEP 3

    Imagine

    Sketch at least two possible solutions. Explain one advantage and one possible weakness of each.

  4. STEP 4

    Plan

    Choose a starting design. Label the parts, list materials, and decide how to measure success.

  5. STEP 5

    Build

    Create a prototype, a first version you can test.

  6. STEP 6

    Test and measure

    Use a consistent testing procedure. Record results, including failures.

    Test the same way every time: same starting point, same load, same distance, same timing.

  7. STEP 7

    Improve

    Use your evidence to choose a change. When possible, change one main feature so you can understand its effect.

  8. STEP 8

    Retest and share

    Compare the improved version with the original. Explain what changed, what worked, and what you would try next.

💡 A design that does not work the first time is useful if it helps you identify what to improve.

🔬 Engineering Challenge or Science Experiment?

An engineering challenge focuses on building a solution to a problem and making it work better.

A controlled science experiment helps answer a question about why a particular feature works, by changing one variable and measuring the result.

The two work together. When you change one main feature between your original and improved designs, your redesign becomes a small experiment.

🏆 What Makes a Strong STEM Project?

  • A clear problem and who it helps
  • Measurable criteria and clear constraints
  • Sketches of more than one idea
  • A consistent, repeatable test
  • At least three trials when practical
  • Honest records, including failures
  • An improvement chosen from evidence

🥽 STEM Project Safety

Every project has its own safety notes. These rules apply to every STEM challenge:

  • Get teacher approval before beginning.
  • Ask an adult to do or supervise cutting thick cardboard, puncturing holes, and using hot glue.
  • Point fans, rubber bands, and moving parts away from faces.
  • Test over a tray or floor area that is clear of people, and wipe up spilled water right away.
  • Keep water away from outlets, cords, and electronics.
  • Never suspend loads over people or stand under something being lifted.
  • Do not eat or taste test materials.
  • Do not use flames, mains electricity, sharp projectiles, or heavy falling objects.
