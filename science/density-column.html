Density Column

Stack six liquids in one glass so they form colorful layers that don't mix, and find out why some liquids sink while others float.

  • TimeAbout 30 min
  • DifficultyEasy
  • Big ideaDensity

🎬 Watch the demo

🧺 Materials

  • 1 tall, clear glass or jar (at least 16 oz)
  • Honey, ¼ cup
  • Light corn syrup, ¼ cup
  • Dish soap, ¼ cup
  • Water, ¼ cup, with a few drops of blue food coloring
  • Vegetable oil, ¼ cup
  • Rubbing alcohol, ¼ cup, with a few drops of red food coloring
  • Measuring cup, a spoon, and paper towels
  • Optional: small test objects such as a grape, a cherry tomato, a cork, and a plastic bead

⚠️ Safety first

  • Wear safety goggles.
  • Rubbing alcohol is flammable. Keep it far away from flames, candles, and stoves.
  • Never taste or drink anything during an experiment, even ingredients from the kitchen.
  • Wipe up spills right away. Oil and soap make floors slippery.
  • At home, do this experiment with an adult.

📋 Steps

  1. Predict. Write down the order you think the liquids will settle in, from bottom to top.
  2. Pour the honey into the center of the glass so it doesn't touch the sides.
  3. Slowly pour the corn syrup on top of the honey.
  4. Slowly add the dish soap. Tip the glass a little and let it run down the inside wall.
  5. Hold a spoon upside down just above the soap. Pour the blue water onto the back of the spoon so it trickles gently down the side.
  6. Use the same spoon trick to add the vegetable oil.
  7. Use the spoon trick one last time to add the red rubbing alcohol. Go slowly, since this layer mixes easily.
  8. Set the glass down and let it sit for a minute. Count the layers you can see.
  9. Optional: Gently drop in your small test objects one at a time. Notice which layer each one stops in.
  10. Clean up. Pour the mixture into a sealed container for the trash rather than down the sink, since oil can clog drains.

🔍 What's happening?

Density is how much matter, or mass, is packed into a certain amount of space, or volume. Every liquid in the glass had the same volume (¼ cup), but each one has a different mass, so each one has a different density.

A denser liquid sinks below a less dense liquid. Honey is the densest, so it ends up on the bottom. Rubbing alcohol is the least dense, so it floats on top. A solid object sinks until it reaches a liquid that is denser than it is, and then it floats there.

Mr. WatfordRemember: Density = mass ÷ volume

💭 Think about it

  1. Did your prediction match the real order of the layers? Which liquid surprised you most?
  2. Why did we pour the liquids slowly and use the back of a spoon?
  3. A grape sank through the oil but stopped on the water. What does that tell you about the grape's density?
