Part A — Key words to know

Netiquette
Using good manners and polite rules when talking with people online.
Cyberbullying
Using technology to hurt, threaten, or embarrass someone again and again.
Phishing
A fake message or website that tries to trick you into giving away private information.
Plagiarism
Copying someone else's words or work and calling it your own.
Personal information
Private facts about you, like your address, phone number, and passwords.
Privacy settings
Controls that decide who can see your posts and information.
AI chatbot
A computer program that answers questions and creates writing or pictures.
Fact-checking
Checking a claim with trustworthy sources to make sure it is true.
Digital footprint
The trail of information you leave behind online (posts, pictures, comments, searches).
Hallucination
When an AI makes up a fact that sounds true but is false.
Deepfake
A fake picture, video, or voice made with AI to look or sound like a real person.

Part C — Staying safe online

  • Never share personal information (home address, phone number, passwords, school) online or with an AI chatbot.
  • Strong passwords are long and mix letters, numbers, and symbols. Weak: password123, 111111, your name.
  • Never share your password, not even with your best friend.
  • Strangers online are not always who they say they are. If someone you don't know asks where you live or go to school, don't answer, block them, and tell a parent.
  • Posts last forever. Even if you delete something, someone may have already taken a screenshot.
  • Your digital footprint matters. Schools and future employers may look at what you have posted. THINK first, use privacy settings, and post only kind, true things.
  • Ask permission before posting a photo of someone else.

Part D — Being kind online & texting

  • Treat people online the same way you would treat them face-to-face.
  • ALL CAPS in a text looks like you are shouting.
  • Texts are easy to misunderstand because the reader can't hear your tone of voice or see your face. Don't assume the worst. Kindly ask, "Hey, is everything okay?" or talk in person.
  • If you see cyberbullying, don't join in or share it. Be kind to the person being hurt and tell a trusted adult. Telling an adult is NOT tattling; it helps keep people safe.
  • If someone sends you an embarrassing photo of someone else, don't share it. Delete it and tell a trusted adult.
  • No texting during class, even if the teacher doesn't see you.
  • Ephesians 4:29 says, "Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying." Online, this means only posting and texting words that build others up: no gossip, insults, or bad language.

Part E — Using AI wisely

  • AI can make mistakes. It is NOT always right. When it makes up a false fact, that is called a hallucination.
  • Fact-check AI by looking it up in a trustworthy source (textbook, encyclopedia, reliable website), comparing more than one source, or asking a teacher or parent.
  • Honest AI use (with your teacher's permission): asking AI to explain something you don't understand, then doing the work yourself.
  • Dishonest AI use: having AI write your essay, copying AI answers onto a test, or turning in AI work as if you wrote it. That is cheating.
  • Deepfakes are fake pictures, videos, or voices made by AI, so don't believe everything you see online.
