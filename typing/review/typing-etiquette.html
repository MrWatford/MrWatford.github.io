Capital letters

  • Capitalize the first word of every sentence.
  • Capitalize names of people, places, days, and titles: Sarah Johnson, Texas, Saturday, Mr. Watford.
  • The word I is always a capital.
  • Don't capitalize words that aren't names: We Went to the Store. → We went to the store.
  • Hold Shift to type a capital letter.

Punctuation and spacing

  • Every sentence ends with a period ., question mark ?, or exclamation point !
  • Questions end with a question mark: What time is lunch?
  • Put one space after a period.
  • Quotation marks: comma before, capital first word inside, period inside: Mom said, "Clean your room."

Complete sentences

  • A complete sentence has a subject and a verb, starts with a capital, and ends with punctuation: The dog chased the ball.
  • A fragment is missing something: Chasing the ball

Professional style

  • Use a respectful, professional tone, not silly, slangy, or dramatic.
  • No texting shortcuts (u, idk, lol) and no emojis. They can make your meaning unclear.
  • Use simple fonts like Arial or Times New Roman.
  • Use paragraphs to separate ideas. Press Enter to start a new paragraph.

Proofreading

  • Proofreading means reading your work to find and fix mistakes.
  • Check capital letters, punctuation, and spelling. (Proofreading doesn't change your topic.)
  • See a mistake while typing? Fix it right away with Backspace.
  • Always proofread one last time before you turn in your work.
